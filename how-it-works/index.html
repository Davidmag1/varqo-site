How Varqo works

Turn autonomous execution into a verified control loop.

Varqo understands what a workflow depends on, verifies the required facts and states independently, controls continuation from evidence, recovers safely when reality diverges, and preserves why each decision was made.

The Varqo control loop

KNOW → VERIFY → CONTROL → RECOVER → PROVE

Authorization is necessary, but it does not prove that the assumptions behind an autonomous action are true. Varqo adds an integrity loop around consequential execution.

The loop is state- and dependency-driven: what must be true for the next step, what is actually true now, and what must happen before execution can safely continue.

Inside VERIFY + CONTROL

Prepare proof early. Bind runtime identity late. Trust neither the agent nor a success label.

Varqo prepares the proof requirement around a consequential action, binds provider-returned runtime identity when needed, executes an independent verifier, and evaluates authoritative observations against the outcome the workflow actually depends on.

A successful tool response is evidence that something was accepted or completed at one layer. Varqo asks whether the downstream condition is actually true.

Recovery

When reality diverges, stopping is only the first safe move.

Recovery restores a state that can be independently proven safe, so autonomous workflows can recover from divergence instead of turning every mismatch into permanent human babysitting.

A recovery action is never a shortcut around verification. It becomes another consequential action whose result must be proven before dependent execution resumes.

Safe retry

Retry when the operation is known to be safe or idempotent and duplication risk is controlled.

Re-read & reconcile

Observe authoritative state again and reconcile when the system is still converging or reality changed outside the agent.

Compensate or roll back

Execute a known corrective action when returning to a safe state requires explicit compensation or rollback.

Human escalation

If automatic recovery cannot be proven safe, keep dependent execution held and require a human decision instead of guessing.

Safety principles

Ambiguity never becomes permission.

The control boundary is designed around independent evidence, explicit dependencies, and fail-closed behavior rather than model confidence.

Independent observation

Varqo queries authoritative state rather than treating the agent's own claim or provider dispatch response as sufficient proof.

Requirement-bound verdicts

Evidence counts only when it proves the specific condition the downstream workflow actually depends on.

Fail closed

Missing plans, verifier failures, malformed evidence, unknown states, wrong dependencies, and exhausted polling do not become permission to continue.

Evidence by design

The Verification Chain preserves what mattered, what was observed, what decision was made, and why execution continued or stopped.
